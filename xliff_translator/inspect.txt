  leaf 0: 'Functional Architecture'
  leaf 1: "Functional architecture in systems engineering is a structured representation of a system’s functions and their interactions, defined independently of the system's physical components. It captures what the system must do to meet its goals and requirements without specifying how these functions are implemented."
  leaf 2: 'The main element of a functional architecture includes system functions, a functional hierarchy that decomposes high-level functions into sub-functions, and functional interfaces that show how different functions interact. The model also captures the flow of operations by defining inputs, outputs, and triggers for each function, ensuring that every interaction is considered.'
  leaf 3: 'In the RFLP (Requirements, Functional, Logical, and Physical) approach, the functional architecture serves as a bridge between requirements and the logical and physical architectures. It defines what the system must accomplish, forming the basis for logical design and physical implementation, ensuring a seamless flow from concept to realization.'
